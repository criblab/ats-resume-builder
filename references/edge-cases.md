# Edge Cases and Special Situations

## Employment gaps

- Use `Month Year – Month Year` for every role. Do not stretch dates; background checks and LinkedIn will contradict you.
- If the gap is short (under 6 months), leave it. Recruiters expect it.
- If it is long, add one honest line under the most recent role or in a short "Career Note" only when it explains something material: caregiving, full-time study, relocation, health, or a layoff.
- Freelance, consulting, and open-source work during the gap count as experience if it is real and relevant. Label it accurately.
- Never fill a gap with a fictional employer or a course presented as a job.

## Career changers

- Lead with a 2-3 line bridge summary: current identity, target identity, proof.
- Put Projects before Experience if the projects are stronger evidence for the target stack.
- Translate outcomes into the target language: "cut reporting time 70%" works for both operations and data engineering.
- Keep a short "Additional Experience" section for unrelated roles instead of giving each one three bullets.
- Expect to explain the pivot in the cover letter; the resume only needs to make it plausible.

Example bridge: "Operations analyst transitioning to backend engineering after shipping three production Django services used by 2,000 employees. Strongest in Python, PostgreSQL, and API design."

## New grads and bootcamp graduates

- Order: Header -> Education -> Projects -> Experience -> Skills (or Skills before Projects if the stack is the strongest signal).
- Internships, part-time jobs, teaching, and open-source count as experience. Use the same XYZ formula.
- Cap at one page. One strong project beats five tutorial clones.
- Include GPA only if above 3.5 and recent; include relevant coursework only if it maps to the JD.
- Projects need a live URL, a repo, and real usage or a clear technical challenge. "Built a to-do app" is not enough; "Built a to-do app with offline sync used by 40 classmates" is.

## Senior and staff candidates

- Two pages is normal; three is almost always too many.
- The summary is worth it: scope, domain, scale, and leadership thesis in three lines.
- Show scope: team size, budget, number of services, traffic, revenue, on-call ownership.
- Show leverage: architecture decisions, migrations, org-wide standards, mentoring, hiring.
- Trim hands-on details that no longer differentiate you. Keep enough to prove you can still build.
- Avoid title inflation. "Staff Engineer" at a 20-person startup is not the same scope as at a 5,000-person company; describe the scope, not just the label.

## Overqualified candidates

- Keep the resume focused on the work the target role actually does.
- Remove or compress director-level scope that signals "too expensive" for the role.
- Address the motivation in the cover letter: why this role, why now, what you want to build.
- Do not hide seniority; explain the fit.

## Short tenures and contracts

- Group contract roles under one "Contract / Freelance" entry with client names and a combined date range.
- If a full-time role lasted under a year, keep it but focus on outcomes and context (layoff, acquisition, contract end).
- Never omit a role that will appear in a background check. Unexplained gaps are safer than discovered omissions.

## International candidates

- State work authorization only when it helps the application. "US citizen" or "Authorized to work in the EU" is useful; "requires sponsorship" is sometimes a knockout, but hiding it wastes everyone's time.
- Use a phone number with country code and a professional email.
- Include a city and country, plus "Open to Remote" or "Open to Relocation" if true.
- For US roles, remove photo, birth date, marital status, nationality, and ID numbers.
- For EU roles, a photo is optional and increasingly discouraged in tech; follow local norms.
- English resume for international companies; native language for local companies. Do not submit a literal translation with awkward phrasing.

## Spanish-language resumes (LATAM / Spain)

- Headers: `EXPERIENCIA LABORAL`, `HABILIDADES TÉCNICAS`, `PROYECTOS`, `EDUCACIÓN`, `CERTIFICACIONES`.
- Keep technology names in English: `Spring Boot`, `Kubernetes`, `CI/CD`.
- Use `A4`, not Letter, for Spain and most of LATAM.
- Dates: `marzo 2022 – presente` or `03/2022 – presente`; be consistent.
- Include `Open to Remote` / `Abierto a remoto` only if true.
- Spanish resumes often include more personal detail; for international companies, keep the US-style privacy rules.

## Non-traditional backgrounds

- Self-taught: lead with projects, contributions, and production evidence; mention the learning path only if it explains depth.
- Military: translate roles into engineering language; include security clearances only if relevant.
- Returners: use a short "Career Note" for the break, then focus on recent projects and refreshed skills.
- PhD/academia: compress publications to the most relevant; lead with applied impact if moving to industry.

## Layoffs and confidentiality

- A layoff is not a performance signal. You can state "role eliminated in a 15% reduction" in one line if asked, not necessarily on the resume.
- Respect NDAs: describe the system and your role without client names, unreleased features, or sensitive metrics.
- Use descriptors such as "a top-5 LATAM marketplace" or "a Fortune 500 insurer" when the employer cannot be named.

## Two-page overflow

Cut in this order:

1. Roles older than 10 years: 1 bullet or a single "Earlier experience" line.
2. Projects that do not support the target.
3. Certifications older than 5 years that are not required.
4. Soft-skill lines and interests.
5. Verbose company descriptions.
6. Font from 10.5pt to 9.8pt (last resort; never below 9pt).
7. Margins from 0.6in to 0.45in (last resort).

Do not cut metrics, the target title, or the most recent role's strongest bullets.

## No metrics available

Use the metric ladder from `SKILL.md`: before/after, absolute scale, relative scope, frequency, time saved, qualitative proof. If nothing is measurable, write the strongest truthful outcome and add `[ADD METRIC: ...]` for the user to fill. Never invent a number to satisfy a rule.
