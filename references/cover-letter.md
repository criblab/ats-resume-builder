# Optional Cover Letter Framework

Most software applications do not need one, but a short, specific letter helps when:

- You are a career changer or returner.
- You are applying to a small company or a founder.
- There is a gap, relocation, or authorization question to address.
- You have a genuine connection to the product or team.

Keep it under 250 words. No greeting template, no "I am writing to express my interest".

## Structure

**Paragraph 1 - The hook (2-3 sentences).**
Name the role, the company, and the single strongest reason you fit. Include one proof point.

**Paragraph 2 - The evidence (3-4 sentences).**
One or two achievements that map directly to the company's problem. Use the same XYZ discipline as the resume, but with more narrative.

**Paragraph 3 - The close (2 sentences).**
Why this company specifically, and a clear next step. No begging, no "I look forward to hearing from you at your earliest convenience".

## Example (fictional)

> I am applying for the Senior Backend Engineer role on the payments platform. I spent the last three years owning settlement services at a payments company, where I cut p95 checkout latency from 840ms to 210ms and led a zero-downtime PostgreSQL migration for a 4.2M-transaction-per-month ledger.
>
> Your team is moving from a monolith to event-driven services while keeping exactly-once semantics. That is the problem I solved at Northwind with Spring Boot, Kafka, and a dual-write migration pattern. I also mentored three engineers through their first on-call rotations, which matters for a team of your size.
>
> I am based in Austin and open to remote. I would be glad to walk through the migration trade-offs and where I would start in the first 90 days.

## Short email version

Subject: Senior Backend Engineer - Alex Rivera

> Hi [Name],
>
> I am applying for the Senior Backend Engineer role. I currently own settlement services at Northwind Payments, where I reduced p95 latency 75% and migrated a 4.2M-transaction ledger to PostgreSQL 16 with zero downtime.
>
> Your event-driven migration is closely aligned with the work I did with Kafka and Spring Boot. I would be happy to share the migration runbook and the trade-offs we tested.
>
> Resume attached. Thanks for your time.
>
> Alex Rivera
> github.com/alexrivera-dev

## Rules

- Research the company: product, recent launch, engineering blog, funding, or a specific technical challenge.
- Do not repeat the resume line by line.
- Do not apologize for gaps or lack of experience; explain and move on.
- Match the language and seniority of the job description.
- Proofread. A typo in a 200-word letter is more visible than in a resume.
- If you use AI to draft it, rewrite it in your own voice and verify every claim.
